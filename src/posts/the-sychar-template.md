Most of us have been taught that evangelism runs on burden. You care enough, so you go. You love enough, so you speak. And when the ground refuses to yield, the conclusion is usually that we did not care enough.

I have come to think that conclusion is wrong — not because burden is unnecessary, but because burden is not the same thing as power.

## The Argument

Soul-burden is not a strategy. Territory we feel burdened for — whether it has never heard of God or has been hearing of Him for generations — requires something beyond a burden for souls. The Sychar outreach must be run by the Holy Spirit leading the charge.

That is the whole claim, and it is a claim about **approach, not terrain**. How much or how little a place already knows is not the argument. Two men in Scripture stand at opposite ends of that spectrum, and both needed the same thing: someone who arrived on the Spirit's instruction, with the tool fitted to what was actually missing.

## The Core Tension

> **Passion measures the depth of the burden. Unction measures the reach of the power.**
> A burden can be sincere and still not be an instrument.

Passion is *about* the lost. Unction is *from* God *toward* the lost. The first can be generated by compassion, guilt, or vision-casting. The second cannot be generated at all — it can only be received, and it can only be received by asking.

This is why the argument insists on Spirit-led rather than Spirit-endorsed. Many outreaches ask God to bless a plan already decided. Sychar asks God for the plan, then walks to the well at the hour He chose.

## What Sychar Actually Establishes

The woman's own words are the pivot:

> "I know that Messiah is coming" (who is called Christ). "When He comes, He will tell us all things." — John 4:25

She had **messianic expectation without messianic encounter**. She was not blank. She had a theological map — Jacob's well, the mountain, the fathers — and an eschatological hope, and a rival claim on worship. What she lacked was the Person.

That distinction matters more than any label we put on the field. The ground was not barren; it was **occupied**. Something was already seated at the centre of her understanding, and it was not Christ.

And this is where the categories break down. Whether we call that ground charted or uncharted changes nothing about what it needed. You do not dislodge a seated occupant by speaking louder. **The missionary task, where an occupant is seated, is not information transfer but displacement** — and displacement is an act of power, not of persuasion.

## The Chain of the Template

John 4 is not a story about a conversation. It is a *protocol* — and each link is reproducible.

| # | Link | Text | What It Establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Divine appointment** | "He needed to go through Samaria" (4:4) | Geography is scheduled, not chosen |
| 2 | **He initiates, He does not perform** | "Give Me a drink" (4:7) | Access is requested, never imposed |
| 3 | **The gift named first** | "If you knew the gift of God" (4:10) | She is addressed as a recipient before she is addressed as a sinner |
| 4 | **Word of knowledge as the doorway** | "you have had five husbands" (4:17–18) | The demonstration is *personal*, not public |
| 5 | **Pivot from natural to spiritual** | "Sir, I perceive that You are a prophet" (4:19) | The sign reclassifies the conversation |
| 6 | **The Father draws** | "the Father is seeking such to worship Him" (4:23) | Conversion is the Father's activity, not ours |
| 7 | **One soul becomes a city** | "Come, see a Man" (4:29) → "many believed" (4:39) | The testimony multiplies without the evangelist |
| 8 | **Second-hand becomes first-hand** | "Now we believe... for we have heard for ourselves" (4:42) | The goal is reached: Christ-centred, not evangelist-centred |

Note the shape of links 4 and 7 together. The demonstration was **one woman deep**, and the harvest was **a city wide**. The power was not scaled to the crowd; it was scaled to the person. That is the opposite of platform evangelism.

Also notice 4:35–38 — "others have laboured, and you have entered into their labours." The woman reaped a field she did not plant. Demonstration makes reapers out of witnesses.

But read that chain carefully, because it is not a script to be repeated everywhere. It is the shape one encounter took when the missing thing was **encounter**. Where the missing thing is **comprehension**, the same Spirit produces a different shape entirely.

## Two Fields, One Spirit — Acts 8

Acts 8 holds both cases in a single chapter, handled by the same evangelist.

**First, Samaria.** Philip goes down and preaches Christ. He casts out unclean spirits, heals the paralysed and the lame — "and there was great joy in that city" (8:5–8). They believe and are baptized.

Yet the Spirit had not fallen on them:

> "For as yet He had fallen upon none of them. They had only been baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus." — Acts 8:16

So the apostles send Peter and John, who pray and lay hands, and they receive the Holy Spirit (8:14–17). Word + demonstration + water baptism + joy — and still incomplete without the empowering. That is the missing link stated twice in the same geography.

**And then Simon.** The same city produced a third response. Simon had amazed Samaria for years with sorcery, and he believed and was baptized when he saw the signs (8:9–13). But when he saw the Spirit given through the laying on of the apostles' hands, he offered them money for the ability (8:18–19). Peter's answer was not gentle: "Your money perish with you" (8:20).

That is the third case in one chapter, and it is the one that should make us careful. **Demonstration does not only produce faith; it attracts appetite.** The same display that turned a city toward Christ drew a man who wanted the power without the Lord. This is why the gift is never for sale and never for show — it is not a commodity, and the vessel is not the point.

**Then, the road to Gaza.** An Ethiopian official, in charge of the treasury of Candace, returning from Jerusalem where he had gone to worship. He is reading Isaiah — and understanding nothing.

> "Do you understand what you are reading?" ... "How can I, unless someone guides me?" — Acts 8:30–31

And Philip does not perform. He does not begin with a word of knowledge, a sign, or a demonstration. He asks a question, accepts an invitation into the chariot, and:

> "beginning at this Scripture, preached Jesus to him." — Acts 8:35

Set the two side by side:

| | Sychar (John 4) | The Ethiopian (Acts 8) |
|---|---|---|
| What the ground already had | Messianic expectation — knowledge without encounter | A text he could not understand |
| What was actually missing | Encounter | Comprehension |
| What the Spirit did | "He needed to go through Samaria" (4:4) | "The Spirit said to Philip, 'Go near'" (8:29) |
| What the minister used | Word of knowledge (4:17–18) | Exposition from Isaiah 53 (8:35) |
| Where the invitation came from | Christ, to the woman | The seeker, to Philip — "come up and sit with me" |
| What was recorded | One soul, then a city | One soul, then a road home rejoicing |
| What was constant | Spirit-initiated approach | Spirit-initiated approach |

**The method is not the template. The leading is.** Philip was the man through whom signs and wonders had just shaken a city, and on the next road he used no sign at all — because there was no occupant to displace, only a text to open. Nobody would call that a failure of unction. It was the same unction, fitted differently.

This is why the argument cannot be built on the terrain. If we decide the approach from the map — "these people are steeped, so we demonstrate; those people are blank, so we explain" — we have made a method out of our own reading of the ground. Both fields in Acts 8 were entered the same way: the Spirit spoke, and a man went.

## The Missing Link — Baptism of Fire

> "He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire." — Matthew 3:11

The instinct is textually sound, and the sequence is instructive:

| Baptism | Agent | Effect | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | The forerunner | Repentance, identification | Matt 3:11 |
| Holy Spirit | Christ | Indwelling, union | Acts 2:4; Rom 8:9 |
| **Fire** | Christ | **Burning, boldness, kindling** | Matt 3:11–12; Acts 2:3; Luke 12:49 |
| Power | Holy Spirit coming upon | **Witness, demonstration** | Acts 1:8 |

**An honest note on the fire.** In the same breath Matthew speaks of the chaff burned "with unquenchable fire" (3:12). Fire in that passage is not merely comfort — it is *purging*. Both readings belong together: fire is what cannot be carried forward being burned away, and what can be carried being kindled. To ask for fire is to ask to be **usable**, and usability always costs the part of us that cannot carry the word. That is why unction is rarer than passion — passion costs time, fire costs the man.

**And the apostles prayed exactly this.** After the first persecution they did not pray for crowds, favour, or open doors:

> "Now, Lord, look on their threats, and grant to Your servants that with all boldness they may speak Your word, by stretching out Your hand to heal, and that signs and wonders may be done through the name of Your holy Servant Jesus." — Acts 4:29–30

Three requests: **boldness**, **a stretched-out hand**, **signs and wonders**. The prayer was answered with a shaking place and a fresh filling (4:31). A church that no longer prays this prayer cannot claim to be continuing the apostolic church — it is continuing a different one.

## The Signs Question — He Rebuked the Demand and Still Met the Need

The nobleman of Capernaum is the hardest case, and it resolves the tension:

> "Unless you people see signs and wonders, you will by no means believe." — John 4:48
> ... "And he himself believed, and his whole household." — John 4:53

He **named the weakness** and then **answered it anyway**. He did not withhold the sign to protect the purity of faith; He gave the sign and let the sign produce the faith. That is mercy, not compromise.

The same pattern, three chapters earlier, with Nathanael:

> "Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you." — John 1:48
> "Because I said to you, 'I saw you under the fig tree,' do you believe? You will see greater things than these." — John 1:50

One private, unverifiable fact — and a sceptic becomes a confessor, who is then promised more. This is the identical mechanism as Sychar: **supernatural knowledge of a person's private life, delivered without accusation.** And John's whole gospel is built on this premise — "these are written that you may believe" (20:30–31). In John, the signs are not decoration on the message. They are the **argument**.

### The necessary distinction

We often negate signs *in order not to appear spooky*. But the thing we are avoiding is not the thing Scripture warns about. The real risk is confusing three things that look alike:

| | Performance | Demonstration | Sign-Demand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motive | To be seen | To answer a situation words cannot | To be convinced |
| Audience | The crowd | The person in front of you | The sceptic setting terms |
| Source | The minister's gift, displayed | The Spirit's leading, calibrated | Coerced |
| Fruit | Admiration of the vessel | Faith in Christ | Nothing — refused |
| Example | — | John 4:17–18; 1:48 | Matt 12:38–39; 16:1–4 |

Jesus refused the third category absolutely and practised the second constantly. "A wicked and adulterous generation seeks after a sign" is not a ban on demonstration — it is a ban on **being commanded by unbelief**.

But note what the same chapter does with the Ethiopian: no demonstration at all, and no rebuke for it. Demonstration is one tool in a fitted hand, not a quota to be met.

## Paul Did Not Minister in Word Only

This is not a departure from apostolic practice — it is the practice:

> "my speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power, that your faith should not be in the wisdom of men but in the power of God." — 1 Corinthians 2:4–5

Note the stated *purpose*: the **location of faith**. Word-only ministry produces faith located in the wisdom of men. Demonstration relocates it into the power of God. That is the whole argument, in Paul's own words.

Confirmed elsewhere: 1 Thessalonians 1:5; Romans 15:18–19; Acts 13:11–12 (Elymas blinded, "then the proconsul believed, when he saw what had been done"); Acts 14:8–10 (Lystra); Acts 16:25–26 (Philippi); Acts 19:11–12 (Ephesus); Acts 28:3–6 (Malta).

Where Paul reasoned without demonstration (Athens, Acts 17), the record is honest: mocking, delay, and "some men joined him and believed" (17:34). It was not nothing. But it was not Sychar.

## Key Distinction

| | Passion-Led Outreach | Spirit-Led Outreach |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | Burden, compassion, urgency | Leading, timing, unction |
| Method | One method, applied everywhere | The method fitted to what is missing |
| Evidence | Sincerity | Demonstration, or exposition — whichever the field requires |
| Address | Broadcast | Personal |
| Origin | Human initiative blessed | Divine appointment obeyed |
| Measure of success | Decisions recorded | First-hand encounter — "we have heard for ourselves" |
| Outcome | Converts to a message | Worshippers of a Person |
| Depletion | Burns the vessel out | Refills the vessel |

## Honest Tensions Kept in View

1. **There is no single apostolic method.** Acts 8 shows the same evangelist using signs in one field and only Scripture in the next. Any teaching that fixes the method has already made a template out of a moment.
2. **Timing is part of the leading.** Jesus forbade the Twelve from Samaritan cities at one stage (Matt 10:5) before opening Samaria at another (Acts 1:8). Timing is part of the approach, not an exception to it.
3. **Fire is not retaliation.** A Samaritan village refused Him, and James and John offered to call fire down (Luke 9:52–54). He rebuked them. The same geography, opposite spirit. Fire is for witness, never for vengeance.
4. **Reason is not the enemy.** The Ethiopian was reached by exposition alone, and Scripture records no sign. The claim is not "no reasoning" — it is "reasoning is not the engine."
5. **False signs exist.** 2 Thessalonians 2:9 warns of signs "according to the working of Satan." The test is not the marvel but the direction: does it terminate on Christ or on the vessel?

## Theological Synthesis

> Passion is the burden that gets you moving.
> The Spirit's instruction is what tells you where.
> The Spirit's gift is what makes the approach supernatural — and the gift is not always a sign.
> Fire is what keeps the vessel usable after the encounter.
> Demonstration turns one testimony into a city; exposition turns a traveller into a believer.
>
> Remove the leading, and the approach still looks like evangelism — it just no longer produces people who say, *"now we believe, not because of your saying."*

## The Approach, as Practice

1. **Ask before going.** Petitions precede plans. The instruction belongs *before* the itinerary, not after the failure.
2. **Pray the Acts 4 prayer, not the attendance prayer** — boldness, a stretched-out hand, signs and wonders.
3. **Expect an instruction, not a campaign.** "He needed to go through Samaria" is a schedule, not a coincidence. "Go near this chariot" is an instruction, not an opportunity.
4. **Diagnose what is missing before choosing the tool.** An occupant must be displaced; a closed text must be opened. Guessing at this is how sincere people arrive with the wrong instrument.
5. **Request access; never impose presence.** "Give Me a drink" came before "I who speak to you am He." "May I come up?" came before Isaiah 53.
6. **Let the demonstration be personal and calibrated** — and let it be absent when it is not needed. Refuse to be commanded by unbelief; refuse to perform on demand.
7. **Aim past the testimony.** The objective is 4:42, not 4:39 — that they stop believing because of you.
